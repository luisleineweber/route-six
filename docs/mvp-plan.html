MVP decisions

Record confirmed choices here before writing the MVP plan or application code.

Confirmed choices

1. First game

  • The MVP supports Pokémon Emerald.
  • The MVP uses one game to check the shared availability rules.
  • Other games and versions can follow later.

2. Progress detail

  • The MVP includes every route and story event.
  • Each route has its own progress point.
  • Gym and League battles remain part of game progress.

3. Evolution level rule

  • Each milestone has a recommended level limit.
  • The limit uses the next Gym or League opponent's highest Pokémon level.
  • The limit applies by default.
  • The user can turn it off to include further level evolutions.

4. Trades

  • The MVP excludes trades with other players.
  • The MVP includes trades with game characters when all requirements are met.
  • Trade availability includes access to the required Pokémon and the character.
  • The MVP has no setting to enable trades with other players.

5. Version rules

  • Show Pokémon that require another game version.
  • Mark them as unavailable in Emerald.
  • Keep this status separate from Pokémon available at a later Emerald milestone.
  • Use Emerald's sources and evolution rules to determine availability.

6. Special sources

  • Include starters, gifts, fossils, and Pokémon the player can obtain only once.
  • Show each source and its requirements.
  • Warn when a team contains choices that cannot be taken together in one playthrough.
  • A Pokémon can be available on its own while its planned team has a choice conflict.

7. Team analysis

  • Use Pokémon types only.
  • Show weaknesses, resistances, immunities, and attack coverage.
  • Moves are outside the MVP scope.

8. Data sources

  • Maintain all Pokémon and Emerald data by hand in local JSON files.
  • Do not add an automated data import or a runtime data API.
  • Check each record against its reference source.
  • Record the reference and review date with the data.

9. Breeding

  • Include breeding in the MVP availability rules.
  • Require Day Care access, a compatible parent pair, and any required held item.
  • Preserve parent sources and choices in the resulting acquisition path.
  • Treat parents and breeding held items as reusable resources.
  • Show breeding requirements and check them in team feasibility results.

Shared rules

Progress uses story milestones. Each available Pokémon needs a valid acquisition path. Team checks combine those paths and check whether all choices can work together.

Progress rules

Each route or story event has a milestone ID. Separate a location from its visits: a route can have an initial visit and a later visit after Surf becomes possible.

Milestones form a dependency graph. previousIds lists mandatory prior milestones. displayOrder controls the list order only. It does not prove access. Selecting a milestone includes its mandatory ancestors. It does not imply that the player visited every optional route shown earlier in the list.

Locations have access requirements. Sources have their own requirements too. For example, reaching a route does not by itself permit its fishing encounters. Fishing equipment, HM access, story gates, and item access must remain explicit.

The planner answers what a player could obtain. It does not track their exact bag, current Pokémon levels, missed catches, or completed optional visits.

Each milestone names its next Gym or League opponent. The stored highest level sets the recommended limit. Before and after a battle are separate milestones. After the final League battle, there is no next League opponent. Show that no recommended limit applies; do not invent a replacement value.

  1. Resolve the selected milestone and its mandatory ancestors.
  2. Resolve accessible locations, item sources, and capabilities.
  3. Seed paths from allowed Pokémon sources. Apply the level rule to source levels.
  4. Follow evolution and breeding rules until no new valid paths appear.
  5. Keep source IDs, parent proofs, minimum levels, choices, and resource uses in paths.
  6. Check future milestones to find the first valid access points for blocked entries.
  7. Return available, later, blocked, or unavailable status with clear reasons.

Use a finite set of useful witness paths. Keep alternatives when they change choices, required resources, access points, or minimum level. Stop cycles; a Pokémon cannot become available merely because a rule needs that same Pokémon.

A level-up evolution requires a level gain. Its minimum output level is the greater of the rule's minimum level and the parent's minimum level plus one. An item evolution keeps the parent's minimum level. Game trades must use their actual level rule; do not assume every received Pokémon has a fixed level.

Evolution conditions include level, stone access, friendship, time, Beauty, stat comparison, personality, and special Emerald conditions. Player trade rules remain visible as blocked rules. They never make a Pokémon available. Do not claim a condition is met unless the game data and rule evaluator prove it.

Available Pokémon remain available through evolution even when the final form has no direct encounter. An unavailable base species does not unlock its family. Turning the level limit off changes level checks only. It does not unlock areas, items, game trades, player trades, or other-version Pokémon.

For each Pokémon, show the valid base source, the location, evolution steps, requirements, and first access points. Do not show an arbitrary first encounter if a different acquisition path is earlier or avoids a choice conflict.

Team choice checks

Individual availability and team feasibility are different results. Keep acquisition alternatives so the team check can find a compatible set of paths.

Check choice groups such as starter selection. Also check finite source uses and finite items consumed by the selected paths. Try all useful path combinations for the six slots before showing a conflict. A shared evolution ancestor alone is not proof of a conflict. A repeatable source can supply more than one Pokémon.

Warn when no compatible path assignment exists. Explain the source, choice, or resource that causes it. Do not erase team members. A future source can remove a current-progress conflict; a whole-game result must therefore check future access.

Breeding parents and held items remain reusable. Combine proofs of repeated parent use instead of counting each egg as a new use of a one-time starter. Two parents must still be distinct Pokémon. A single finite source cannot supply both parents when it provides only one Pokémon. Keep gender assignments and resource limits consistent across the whole plan. A parent can breed before a later evolution or game trade; the team check must find a valid order for those actions.

Breeding rules

Include breeding when the Day Care, two compatible parents, and required held items are accessible. Check actual possible genders for each acquisition path. Do not use a species-wide gender list when a particular gift or trade fixes gender. Keep reviewed gender transitions through evolutions and separately created products. Parent access must be possible under the selected progress and level settings.

Use the selected game's Egg Groups and compatibility rules. Handle Ditto and non-breeding species explicitly. The Emerald Day Care code defines compatibility, offspring, and incense conditions.

Maintain offspring rules by hand in breeding-rules.json. Each rule names the eligible family parents, possible offspring species, and required held item. The module finds a compatible partner from accessible Pokémon profiles. Do not enter every possible species pair. Each result must include a concrete parent pair with valid acquisition proofs.

Keep ordinary egg gifts separate from breeding sources. For Emerald, use its actual hatch level. Apply the level limit to both parent access and the hatched Pokémon. Show parents, Day Care access, required items, and hatch level in details. Inherited moves and breeding for battle stats remain outside the type-only MVP.

Breeding can permit several forms of one starter family. Each offspring keeps the original starter choice. A breeding cycle with no accessible parents does not unlock a species. Preserve these proofs when resolving later evolutions.

Type analysis

Use Emerald's Pokémon type profiles and its type chart. Shared Pokémon identity data contains names and Dex numbers; it does not impose modern types on old games. For example, Emerald's Ralts profile is Psychic. See the Emerald species data.

For each attacking type, multiply its chart entries against both defending types. Count values greater than 1 as weaknesses, values between 0 and 1 as resistances, and 0 as immunities. Keep 2× and 4× weaknesses visible in Pokémon details.

For attack coverage, use each distinct team type as an attacking type. A defending type is covered when at least one team type deals more than 1× damage to it. The denominator is the selected game's active type count. It is not fixed at 18. Show repeated types separately. Do not imply that a Pokémon has learned a move.

Manual data and clear module boundaries

The data contract specifies all files and references. The TypeScript contract defines input and result types. The JSON Schema checks the assembled dataset.

Write and check data in story sections. Start with the route to the first Gym. Then add each section through the League and normal post-game areas. Each complete section must work before the next one is added. The MVP is complete only when the agreed full route and event coverage is present.

Keep a reference and review date for each record. Check the original game and its source data. The Emerald decompilation is a reference for manual review, not an automated import. Use a recorded revision when checking data. Correct errors in the maintained JSON files.

Validate JSON shape first. Then check IDs, graph cycles, references, type-chart dimensions, version types, source levels, choice limits, and catalogue coverage. Missing data is a data error. It must not silently become an unavailable Pokémon.

Shared identity and game rules

Shared Pokémon records contain ID, National Dex number, name, introduction generation, and review information. Game profiles contain types, Egg Groups, and possible genders. Game evolution records contain version-specific rules. This avoids assigning later-generation types or evolutions to Emerald.

The initial catalogue covers species present in Generation III. Every selected game profile must refer to a shared species. Later games can add profiles and rules without changing the identity record or the availability interface.

The type chart stores a complete matrix. Row and column order follow typeIds. Each cell is explicit, including neutral damage. Multiply two cells for a dual type. Do not fill missing cells with neutral damage.

Milestones and access

previousIds records mandatory milestone dependencies. displayOrder only sorts the picker. locationId can be null for a story event. nextBossId can be null when no next Gym or League opponent exists.

Requirements are tagged values: all, any, milestone, capability, item, or Pokémon. An empty all means no requirement. An empty any is invalid. An item or Pokémon requirement describes access, not actual player inventory. The acquisition path must preserve the witness source and its finite uses.

Capabilities name game actions such as Surf access or a fishing method. Their requirements prove access from story progress and obtainable items. Do not make a capability available solely because an unrelated route occurs earlier in a list.

Sources and levels

Each source names a Pokémon, location, requirement, optional choice, finite-use limit, level rule, possible genders, details, and review. Null maxUses means repeatable access. Positive maxUses limits uses in one playthrough.

Level rules are fixed, range, or match the Pokémon offered in a game trade. The last rule must name the offered species. The source details must name the same offered species. The validator checks this equality.

Wild details include method and encounter rate. Other source kinds are starter, gift, static encounter, egg gift, fossil revival, and game trade. Fossils name the consumed item. Game trades name the offered Pokémon. Those requirements must also appear in the requirement tree so they cannot be ignored.

Source genders describe the outcomes possible from that source. They must form a non-empty subset of the species profile's possible genders. Track gender through each evolution's reviewed gender transitions. A source with a fixed gender must not permit the profile's other genders.

Breeding sources and rules

Breeding is a source kind. Its details name ruleId and capabilityId. The capability proves Day Care access and must also appear in the requirement tree. Breeding sources use a fixed hatch level and null maxUses for repeatable eggs. Emerald eggs hatch at level 5; see the game constant.

A breeding rule names parentPokemonIds, offspringPokemonIds, and an optional requiredHeldItemId. Parent IDs identify the family parent that determines the offspring. The module finds its partner through profile Egg Groups and genders. An offspring source must name an offspring declared by its rule.

Use Emerald's compatibility and offspring rules. Normal pairs need opposite genders and a shared Egg Group. Ditto pairs follow the game's distinct rule. Reject two Ditto and any parent in the Undiscovered group. Keep special offspring alternatives explicit in the rule data. An incense rule needs its held item as an accessible, non-consumed item requirement.

Resolve two distinct parent Pokémon with compatible acquisition paths. Check source gender restrictions, choices, finite sources, and parent level limits. Parents are not consumed. The same pair can support later eggs. A retained parent proof must not count as a new capture for every egg. Check copies needed together and the action order before declaring the entire team feasible.

Breeding paths retain all parent source IDs, chosen genders, parent path IDs, choices, and resource uses. Add a path only after its parent proofs are grounded. Do not create access from a self-dependent breeding rule. Apply later evolution rules to each hatched species as normal.

Evolution rules

Evolution triggers are level-up, use-item, and player-trade. A level-up needs a level gain even when the source already exceeds the nominal minimum level. Use-item needs an item ID and item access. Player-trade rules are blocked in this MVP. A held item on a trade rule does not remove that restriction.

Conditions describe friendship, Beauty, time, stat comparison, personality, and spare party slots. Item and action access remain in the requirement tree. coProductIds records simultaneous products when the game creates more than one Pokémon. Team checks must not count that outcome as two independent uses of a finite source. Check these details against the selected game.

Null genderTransitions means the evolution preserves gender. Otherwise, each entry records a possible input and output gender. Validate those entries against the two species profiles and the actual source restrictions. Do not assume every evolution or separately created product preserves gender.

Record the actual condition. Do not replace a rule with a blanket statement that all non-level evolutions are available.

Exclusions and coverage

Use an explicit exclusion for a species that needs another version or an external distribution event. A player-trade-only form is explained by its blocked evolution path. An exclusion is not a substitute for missing encounter or evolution data.

Every profile must have at least one declared source, an incoming evolution path, or an explicit exclusion. Validate that the chain has a grounded outcome. A reference cycle with no base source does not establish availability.

Reviews and reference sources

Each record has a review date and reference IDs. Each reference has a title and locator. A URL and a source revision are optional when the reference is direct game observation. Record a repository revision when using source files.

Use the original game and manually inspect the Emerald source reconstruction. Useful reference areas are species profiles, evolution rules, wild encounters, and trainer parties. Breeding also needs Day Care rules and hatch constants. Location and event access also need script review or direct game checks.

These are review sources. The application reads only the maintained local data. No external API or import script forms part of this plan.

Required semantic checks

  • IDs are unique within their collections. All cross-references resolve.
  • National Dex numbers are unique. Profiles do not predate their species.
  • Milestone dependencies are acyclic. Each milestone is rooted at game start.
  • Display orders are unique. Each route has an initial-access milestone.
  • All source locations, capabilities, items, Pokémon, and choices exist.
  • Required item and offered Pokémon dependencies match their source details.
  • Source and evolution levels lie from 1 to 100; ranges have minimum <= maximum.
  • The first milestone exists. Boss references exist and supply the displayed cap.
  • Every active type has exactly one matrix row and column. Profiles use active types.
  • Emerald's chart and profiles use its Generation III rules.
  • Choice limits do not exceed their option count. Choice references use real options.
  • Profile Egg Groups are recognised. Source genders match their profiles.
  • Breeding source rule and capability IDs resolve. The offspring belongs to its rule.
  • Breeding sources are repeatable, use a fixed hatch level, and require Day Care access.
  • Emerald breeding sources use hatch level 5. Incense requirements retain the held item.
  • Parent pairs have grounded, compatible proofs and enough distinct Pokémon copies.
  • Evolution outputs differ from inputs. Evolution and capability paths have a grounded source; cycles do not manufacture access.
  • Every catalogue entry has a reviewed outcome. Each explicit exclusion has a reason.
  • Review references exist; dates are valid. A source error fails validation visibly.

Use explicit validation results with record IDs and field paths. Invalid data must stop the affected game from loading and show a clear data error.

Domain glossary

Pokémon Playthrough Planner

The planner helps players choose a team and check which Pokémon they can obtain at a selected point in a game.

Language

*Milestone*: A point in game progress, such as a story event or the point before a Gym battle. _Avoid_: Badge count as the name for all progress.

*Badge*: A reward from a Gym battle. Badge count describes only part of game progress.

*Team*: The set of up to six Pokémon that a player chooses for a playthrough.

*Level limit*: The recommended maximum Pokémon level at a milestone. It determines which level evolutions the planner considers available when the limit applies. _Avoid_: Level cap.

*Game trade*: A Pokémon exchange with a character in the selected game. _Avoid_: Trade without a named kind.

*Player trade*: A Pokémon exchange with another player.

*Choice conflict*: A team includes Pokémon whose required choices cannot all be made in one playthrough.

*Availability*: Whether a player could obtain a Pokémon at a milestone under the selected rules. It does not mean that the player already owns that Pokémon.

*Acquisition path*: A source and the steps needed to obtain a Pokémon from it.

*Source*: A way to obtain a Pokémon, such as a wild encounter, gift, fossil revival, game trade, or breeding.

*Capability*: A game action that the player can use after meeting its requirements, such as Surf or a fishing method.

*Breeding*: The production of an egg from two compatible Pokémon at the Day Care.

*Parent pair*: Two distinct Pokémon used together to produce an egg. A compatible pair meets the selected game's species, gender, and Egg Group rules.

*Egg Group*: A group that determines which Pokémon can breed together under the game's rules.
